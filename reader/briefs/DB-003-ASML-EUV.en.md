# Headline

ASML’s same-day announcements put the first NXE:3100 EUV systems with customers — but at T0 the durable value story is still immersion extension, not a finished EUV transition.

---

# Executive Summary

Why should I care? On 22 February 2010, ASML said TSMC would install an NXE:3100 EUV system for R&D, reported the 100th XT:19x0 immersion shipment and NXT ramp, and highlighted FlexRay illuminators with source-mask optimisation (SMO). Continued shrink via immersion is costly and slow under multi-patterning pressure; EUV is framed as a potential cost reducer, while immersion extension is what is shipping. If the near-term scarce layer is the coupled scanner–illuminator–SMO stack that makes multi-exposure manufacturable, ASML sits at the centre of that portfolio. What we do **not** know is whether production EUV will raise or lower lithography-layer value, or what prices and the other five NXE recipients imply. Those items remain Unknown.

*(Derived from SRE-106 §§1, 6–7. Reasoned Inference, Medium; EUV value-change direction Unknown.)*

# What Changed?

On 22 February 2010, ASML published three related releases.

- TSMC is to receive an NXE:3100 EUV lithography system for R&D use (ASML states six NXE:3100 development tools in the program).
- ASML reports shipment of the 100th TWINSCAN XT:19x0 immersion system and continued NXT platform ramp for high-productivity immersion.
- FlexRay programmable illuminators and SMO are positioned to help customers extend immersion under multi-patterning constraints (overlay, CDU, throughput).
- EUV is discussed as a potential path to lower cost versus stretching immersion further; production timing and performance commitments are not established as closed facts at T0.

*(Facts only — SRE-106 §2.)*

---

# Why It Matters

Leading-edge shrink still has to be manufacturable. Immersion faces low-k1 limits and multi-patterning that demand tighter overlay/CDU and very high throughput, plus long custom illuminator cycles. Buyers need a portfolio that keeps immersion viable while EUV develops.

ASML’s answer at T0 is not “EUV replaces everything today.” It is a coupled stack:

1. **Immersion productivity** — XT/NXT systems shipping at volume.
2. **Programmable illumination + SMO** — tools that bind scanner behaviour to multi-exposure manufacturability.
3. **EUV as development placement** — NXE:3100 tools with customers for R&D, not yet a proven production transition.

Whether EUV ultimately raises or lowers the economic value of the lithography layer is still Unknown.

*(SRE-106 §§3–4, 6.)*

---

# Where Value Is Moving

Simple value flow (five nodes):

```text
[Single-exposure immersion shrink]
        ↓
[Multi-patterning overlay / CDU / throughput pressure]
        ↓
[High-productivity immersion scanners (XT / NXT)]
        ↓
[FlexRay illuminators + SMO as coupled portfolio]
        ↓
[EUV NXE development tools at customer sites]
```

**Reading.** Near-term value pressure moves **toward** the supplier that owns scanner, programmable illuminator, and SMO as one portfolio for multi-exposure manufacturing. ASML is that supplier on immersion evidence. EUV’s effect on lithography-layer value — up or down — remains Unknown.

*(SRE-106 §§5–7. Potential reallocation on immersion; Medium. EUV direction Unknown.)*

# Reality Check

**Current thesis.** At T0, durable capture sits with immersion extension — scanner plus illuminator plus SMO — and ASML is structurally placed there; EUV placements matter for the path ahead, but whether EUV raises or lowers lithography value is Unknown.

**What would prove us wrong?**

- A rival multi-patterning scanner matching overlay, CDU, and throughput without ASML’s coupled stack.
- Surplus accruing to buyers or optics/source suppliers rather than the scanner vendor.
- Production EUV ending multi-exposure **and** ASML losing the EUV position.
- Leading-edge manufacturing proceeding without the immersion-extension portfolio.

**Next validation point.** Further NXE installs; immersion margins and NXT/FlexRay volume (including Q2 2010 windows called out in SRE-106); competitor EUV or multi-patterning announcements; and customer choices that treat EUV as a production tool rather than R&D only.

*(SRE-106 §§9–10.)*

---

# Bottom Line

The headline news is EUV tools at customers; the T0 capture story is still immersion extension. ASML is the clearest structural beneficiary on that immersion portfolio. Prices, other NXE recipients, and whether EUV increases or decreases lithography-layer value remain Unknown — so the near-term mechanism is clearer than the long-run size of anyone’s gain.

*(SRE-106 key conclusion.)*
