## 1. Executive Summary

Apple announced the App Store opening with more than 500 native applications from hundreds of companies, downloadable wirelessly over cellular networks and Wi-Fi directly onto the device, charged to the user's iTunes account, and present as a built-in application on every iPhone running iPhone 2.0 software [Apple, press release, 2008-07-10]. The company's own framing of the announcement is not about the handset: the release describes iPhone as "a new software platform for developers, combining the most advanced mobile operating system, sophisticated developer tools and a breakthrough way for developers to wirelessly sell and distribute their applications right onto every iPhone," and the subhead leads with the application count rather than with the 3G radio or the GPS receiver. What changes is where third-party software meets the customer: through a channel the platform owner builds into every device and bills through its own account system. *(Evidence-supported for the change; Reasoned Inference for its significance)*

**Key conclusion.** Value migrates from handset hardware as the locus of differentiation, and from services delivered to phones by other means, toward the application distribution layer and the account system that settles payment for it — a layer Apple owns, places by default on every device, and intermediates every disclosed transaction through. *(Reasoned Inference, Medium)*

**Tier 1 is assigned, but its magnitude is not established.** Apple occupies the position: the store is built into every iPhone running the new software, Apple describes itself as supplying the means by which developers sell and distribute, and payment runs through the user's iTunes account [Apple, press release, 2008-07-10]. What the document does not contain is any commercial term — no developer revenue share, no commission, no developer program fee, no review or approval process, and no statement that the store is the only route to an iPhone user. The position is therefore evidenced while the share retained is **Unknown**. A further fact cuts against early monetization: more than 125 of the more than 500 launch applications are free. *(Reasoned Inference for the position, Medium; Unknown for the share)*

**Top companies for further research — not recommendations, not ranked by attractiveness.** This list identifies where the reasoning in §§6–7 lands. It contains no view on valuation or timing.

- **Apple** — owns the distribution layer, the account system that settles payment, the operating system, and the developer tools, as one bundle. Tier 1; share retained Unknown. *(Reasoned Inference, Medium)*
- **AT&T** — the device requires a new two-year AT&T contract in the US, with the rate plan sold separately, and the store operates over cellular networks. Tier 2. *(Reasoned Inference, Low)*
- **eBay** — quoted stating iPhone had quickly become its number one mobile device and that the equivalent experience could not be delivered on any other mobile device. Tier 3. *(Reasoned Inference, Low)*

Market blind spot: attention at T0 attaches to the application count and to the 3G radio, while the load-bearing detail is that the store is a built-in application on every device running the new software and that every charge settles against an account Apple already holds. Placement and billing are the parts of this announcement that are hard for another party to replicate; the catalogue is not. *(Reasoned Inference, Medium)*

## 3. Customer Problem

**What end-user problem changed.** For the customer, obtaining and installing software on a phone became a wireless, on-device, immediate action. The source states applications download directly onto the iPhone with the customer able to "start using them immediately," that the store works over cellular networks and Wi-Fi and is "accessible from just about anywhere," and that the store notifies the user when updates are available [facts 4, 12, 14]. The alternative path is also disclosed and is slower: the store in iTunes on a Mac or PC, synced to the device over a USB cable [fact 15]. *(Evidence-supported)*

For the developer — the party the release addresses most directly — the problem that changed is reaching and charging the customer. The source describes "a breakthrough way for developers to wirelessly sell and distribute their applications right onto every iPhone" [fact 9], and states that applications are free or charged to the user's iTunes account [fact 13]. Distribution and payment collection are supplied to the developer rather than assembled by it. *(Evidence-supported)*

The source also discloses a capability problem on the demand side, expressed through partner statements: the AP describes delivering news "in a way that simply wasn't possible before" with offline reading, eBay states it "couldn't deliver such a complete and easy-to-use eBay experience on any other mobile device," and Sega describes touch and tilt controls opening games to "an entirely new audience" [facts 17, 18, 21]. What these have in common is a service that existed but could not be delivered to a phone in a form its provider considered adequate. *(Reasoned Inference)*

**A limitation to record rather than paper over.** The release contains no description of how mobile software was previously distributed, no named prior channel, and no account of what it cost a developer to reach a phone user before this. Unlike a source that states the prior state explicitly, this one asserts a "breakthrough" without characterising what it breaks from. The magnitude of the customer problem, and the identity of what is displaced, are therefore **Unknown**.

## 4. Industry Change

**The concrete change.** A platform owner opens an application distribution channel that is built into every device running its new software, reachable wirelessly from almost anywhere, and settled through an account system it already operates [facts 8, 12, 13]. *(Evidence-supported)*

**Which system requirement shifts.** Three requirements move together in the source:

1. **Where software meets the customer.** Distribution moves onto the device itself, over the cellular network and Wi-Fi, with immediate use after download [facts 4, 12]. A desktop and cable path remains available but is the secondary one in the release's own ordering [fact 15]. *(Evidence-supported)*
2. **Who holds the payment relationship.** Applications are free or charged to the user's iTunes account [fact 13]. The customer's payment credentials sit with the platform owner, not with the developer. *(Evidence-supported)*
3. **What the device is sold as.** The release frames iPhone as a software platform, pairing the operating system, developer tools, and the selling and distribution mechanism as one offering [fact 9], and the subhead leads with the application count rather than with hardware [facts 1, 5]. The platform extends beyond the phone: iPhone 2.0 is free for iPhone customers and $9.95 for iPod touch users [facts 26, 27]. *(Reasoned Inference)*

**Why the prior solution becomes insufficient.** The disclosed capabilities are not incremental for the services described. Offline-readable news, a complete auction-marketplace client, and games driven by touch and tilt controls each depend on a native application with access to the display, Multi-Touch input, hardware-accelerated 3D graphics, the accelerometer, and location technology [facts 11, 17, 18, 21]. Whatever delivery method the quoted partners used before, three of them state on the record that what they are now shipping was not previously possible or not possible elsewhere. A distribution channel is what converts that capability into something a customer can obtain. *(Reasoned Inference)*

**Structural or cyclical.** Structural. The change alters what participants must do: a developer wanting to reach these customers ships through a channel the platform owner supplies and bills through an account the platform owner holds [facts 9, 13]; a customer acquires software on the device rather than through a computer [facts 4, 12]. This is a change in the mechanism of exchange, not in its volume. *(Reasoned Inference)*

**What the source does not settle.** The release does not state that the App Store is the only way to install an application on an iPhone [see the §2 Unknown list]. It states that the store is built into every iPhone running iPhone 2.0 and that the device runs applications available through it [facts 7, 8], which establishes default and universal placement but not exclusivity. Whether the channel is the sole route or merely the default is **Unknown**, and the distinction matters to every conclusion that follows.

## 6. Value Migration

**Purpose.** State where value moves, and why it must. Origins and destinations use the layers established in §5.

**Where value moves — origin and destination both named.**

| From (origin layer) | To (destination layer) | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device hardware, as the locus of what differentiates the product | Mobile operating system, developer tools, and application distribution, presented as one platform offering | Facts 1, 9, 26, 27 |
| Third-party application development, insofar as it held the customer relationship and collected payment itself | Payment and account settlement, where charges are made against the user's iTunes account | Facts 9, 13 |
| Desktop software and device sync, as the route by which software reaches the device | Application distribution and discovery on the device, over cellular networks and Wi-Fi, with immediate use | Facts 4, 12, 15 |
| Content and service publishers' own delivery to phones, which three partners state was inadequate or impossible elsewhere | Native applications on the device, using display, Multi-Touch, 3D graphics, accelerometer, and location technology | Facts 11, 17, 18, 21 |

**Why the migration follows.** Step by step, so any single link can be attacked:

1. The services described require capabilities that are specific to the device and its native application model — offline-readable news, a complete marketplace client, touch-and-tilt games — and three partners state on the record that these were not previously possible, or not possible on any other mobile device [facts 17, 18, 21]. *(Evidence-supported that the source makes these claims)*
2. If a service can only be delivered as a native application, the developer needs a way to place that application on the customer's device and to be paid for it. *(Reasoned Inference)*
3. The source shows both functions supplied by the platform owner as one offering: distribution through a store built into every device running the new software, and payment charged to an account the platform already holds [facts 8, 9, 13]. *(Evidence-supported)*
4. Where distribution and payment are supplied by the same party that controls the operating system and default placement, the position that determines whether a developer reaches a customer moves to that party. *(Reasoned Inference)*
5. Therefore value reallocates from hardware differentiation and from the developer's own route to the customer, toward the distribution and settlement layers of the platform. *(Reasoned Inference)*

**Two links are explicitly not established.** Step 4 does not require exclusivity, but its strength depends on it: the source states default and universal placement, not sole access [facts 7, 8], so how much of the migration is compelled rather than merely convenient is **Unknown**. Step 5 says nothing about magnitude, because no commercial term appears anywhere in the document [see the §2 Unknown list]; with more than 125 of more than 500 launch applications free [facts 1, 2], the reallocated amount at T0 may be small even if the direction is right.

**A structural limitation of this case.** The origin side of the largest claimed migration — distribution — cannot be named from this source, which is why §5 carries a `Prior mobile software distribution` row with every cell `Unknown`. The migrations tabulated above are the ones whose origins the source does establish: hardware, developer-held payment, desktop sync, and publishers' prior delivery. A claim that value moved away from a specific prior channel would require a document this report does not have. *(Unknown)*

**Confidence on the migration as a whole: Medium.** *(Reasoned Inference)* The direction is well supported, because the platform owner states the bundling of operating system, tools, distribution, and selling in its own words [fact 9] and the mechanics of placement and billing are disclosed [facts 8, 13]. The magnitude is undisclosed, exclusivity is unstated, and the prior state is unspecified. Under `VALUE_CAPTURE_METHODOLOGY.md` §6, undisclosed load-bearing elements cap confidence below High.

**Statement of standing.** Potential value reallocation. Requires later market verification.

## 7. Value Capture

Per `VALUE_CAPTURE_METHODOLOGY.md`: value creation is not value capture, revenue growth is not value capture, and technology leadership is not value capture. That more than 500 applications reached customers who could not obtain them this way before is a *creation* claim [facts 1, 16, 17, 18]; the question here is who retains it.

### Tier 1 — Direct Value Capturer

**Apple — Tier 1, Direct Value Capturer.** *(Reasoned Inference, Medium)*

Retention mechanism, which the methodology requires to be named, and which this source does supply in three parts:

- **Default and universal placement.** The App Store is a built-in application on every iPhone running iPhone 2.0 software [fact 8]. A competing channel cannot obtain that position for itself, and the source states the software update is free for iPhone customers [fact 26], which removes the friction to the placement spreading across the installed base.
- **Intermediation of the sale.** Apple describes its own contribution as "a breakthrough way for developers to wirelessly sell and distribute their applications right onto every iPhone" [fact 9]. The selling mechanism is the platform's, not the developer's.
- **Ownership of the payment relationship.** Applications are free or charged to the user's iTunes account [fact 13]. The credentials that make a purchase possible are already held by the platform owner, which is a position built before this announcement and reused by it.

Scarcity source under `VALUE_CAPTURE_METHODOLOGY.md` §4 Step 2: default placement on the device plus an existing billing relationship with the customer. Neither is replicable by a party that does not control the operating system, and §5 marks both layers `Scarcity: Yes`. The bundle is completed by the operating system and developer tools, which the release presents as part of the same platform [fact 9].

**What is not established, and it is the magnitude.** The document contains no revenue share, no commission, no developer fee, no application price, and no statement of exclusivity [see the §2 Unknown list]. More than 125 of the more than 500 launch applications are free [facts 1, 2], so a substantial share of the initial catalogue generates no transaction to divide. The position is therefore evidenced while the share of economic profit retained is **Unknown**. Confidence is Medium on the position and the mechanism; the magnitude is not assessed at any confidence level, because the source contains nothing to assess.

This is the reverse of the situation in a case where terms are published and position is unclear. Here the structure is visible and the economics are absent.

### Direct beneficiaries

**AT&T — Tier 2, Direct Beneficiary.** *(Reasoned Inference, Low)*

First-order economics improve, and the mechanism is disclosed rather than inferred: in the US the device is sold in AT&T retail stores and requires a new two-year contract, with the rate plan sold separately to qualified customers [facts 24, 25]. A two-year commitment is a switching cost that accrues to the carrier, and the store operating over cellular networks [fact 12] gives the carrier a usage-side connection to application adoption.

Retention is partial and its scope is narrow on this evidence. The contract requirement is disclosed only for the US, while the device launches in 21 countries [fact 28] with no carrier arrangement stated for the other 20 [see the §2 Unknown list]. No revenue-sharing or economic term between Apple and AT&T appears in the document. Whether AT&T retains a disproportionate share is **Unknown**, and confidence is Low because the disclosed mechanism covers one of twenty-one markets.

### Indirect beneficiaries

**eBay — Tier 3, Indirect Beneficiary.** *(Reasoned Inference, Low)* Second-order path, stated in full: eBay gains a delivery channel for its marketplace, and its representative states that iPhone "has quickly become the number one mobile device for accessing eBay," that the experience could not be delivered on any other mobile device, and that the application is free to download [fact 18]. It occupies the content and service publishers layer, which §5 marks `↑` on `Hypothesis`. No retention mechanism is identified: a free application that improves access to an existing marketplace may increase transaction volume, but the source discloses nothing about whether that gain is retained. Under `VALUE_CAPTURE_METHODOLOGY.md` §6, exposure without a named retention mechanism is not capture, which is why this is Tier 3 rather than Tier 2.

**Other named publishers — Tier 3, not entered in §8.** The Associated Press, Facebook, MySpace, Sega, and Travelocity are each quoted describing a service delivered through the store [facts 17, 19, 20, 21, 22]. Their paths are the same in kind as eBay's. None is entered in §8, for the listing reasons stated there, and no parent company is inferred for any of them. *(Reasoned Inference, Low)*

### Potential beneficiaries

**Third-party developers as a layer — Tier 4, Potential Beneficiary, unattributed.** *(Hypothesis, Low)* Hundreds of companies shipped more than 500 applications at launch [fact 16], gaining distribution and payment collection they did not previously have from this party [fact 9]. Whether they retain value depends entirely on commercial terms the source does not disclose, which is why §5 marks the layer `Unknown` rather than `↑`. Resolving condition, routed to §10: publication of the revenue share and developer program terms.

**Device customers — Tier 4, Potential Beneficiary, unattributed.** *(Hypothesis, Low)* `VALUE_CAPTURE_METHODOLOGY.md` §4 Step 4 requires the buyer to be considered as a candidate capturer. More than 125 free applications at launch [fact 2] and a free software update for existing iPhone customers [fact 26] are surplus delivered to the customer on the source's own terms. Against that, the US device requires a two-year contract [facts 24, 25] and the update costs $9.95 on iPod touch [fact 27]. The net position of the customer is **Unknown**.

### The central open question

The structure of capture is disclosed and the economics of capture are not. Placement, intermediation, and billing are all stated [facts 8, 9, 13]; revenue share, commission, application prices, and exclusivity are all absent [see the §2 Unknown list]. The direction is therefore Medium-confidence and the division of spoils is not established at any confidence level. Consistent with the methodology, the possibility that most surplus accrues to developers and customers at T0 — with more than a quarter of the launch catalogue free — is not excluded by this source.

## 9. Risks

### Assumptions that could fail

1. **The store is the route to the customer, not merely the default one** — supports §5, §6 Step 4, and the §7 Tier 1 assignment. The source states the store is built into every device running the new software and that the device runs applications available through it [facts 7, 8], but never states exclusivity [see the §2 Unknown list]. If an alternative route to the same customers exists or emerges, the `Bottleneck: Yes` assessment weakens to `No` and the Tier 1 mechanism reduces to a strong default position. *(Unknown at T0)*
2. **Commercial terms allow the platform to retain a share** — supports the whole of §7. No revenue share, commission, or fee appears in the document. If terms transfer most of the surplus to developers, the Tier 1 position is real while its economic content is small. *(Unknown)*
3. **A paid catalogue develops** — supports §§6–7. More than 125 of more than 500 launch applications are free [facts 1, 2], and no application price appears anywhere in the release. If the catalogue remains predominantly free, distribution is valuable strategically while reallocating little measurable value. *(Reasoned Inference)*
4. **Native applications are genuinely necessary for these services** — supports §6 Steps 1–2. The evidence is six partner statements selected and published by the platform owner [facts 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22]. If comparable service quality becomes reachable by other means on the device, the compulsion behind the migration weakens. *(Hypothesis)*
5. **Adoption is not gated by the carrier arrangement** — supports §§6–8. The US device requires a new two-year contract with a separately sold rate plan [facts 24, 25], and the iPod touch route to the same software costs $9.95 [fact 27]. Terms in the other twenty launch markets are undisclosed [fact 28]. If contract requirements suppress device adoption, the distribution layer's reach is smaller than universal placement implies. *(Unknown)*
6. **Developers continue to participate under undisclosed terms** — supports §5's `Unknown` on that layer. Participation at launch is evidenced by hundreds of companies [fact 16]; continued participation depends on terms not in the document, including any review process, which is unstated. *(Unknown)*

### What would weaken the thesis

- Commercial terms proving generous to developers, leaving the direction of the migration intact while the share retained by the platform is modest.
- The catalogue remaining predominantly free, so that a distribution position of real strategic value reallocates little measurable economic value.
- Device adoption constrained by contract requirements in some or all of the launch markets, reducing the reach of default placement.
- Other platform owners establishing comparable stores, so that a distribution channel becomes an expected platform feature rather than a differentiator.

### What would invalidate the thesis

- **Applications reaching iPhone customers at scale through a route the platform owner does not control**, which removes the bottleneck the §5 assessment and the Tier 1 mechanism both rest on.
- **Economic profit accruing to the publishers and developers rather than the platform**, if terms leave the surplus with the parties that supply the applications, in which case the Tier 1 designation is mislocated and the correct finding is that surplus passes through to the layers that create it.
- **Native application delivery failing to displace the alternatives**, with customers continuing to reach these services by other means on the device, which invalidates the migration rather than reducing it.
- **The payment relationship proving not to matter**, if customers transact with developers directly without friction, removing one of the three parts of the disclosed retention mechanism.

Weakening and invalidating conditions are kept separate deliberately: the first four reduce the size of the conclusion, the last four replace it.
